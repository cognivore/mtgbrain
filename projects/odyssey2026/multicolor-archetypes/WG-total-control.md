# {W}{G} Selesnya — Total Control

A defensive go-long Selesnya deck that locks the board, taps and fogs, builds a token wall, gains life to survive, and grinds out a slow, honest win. Existing payoffs: Phantom Nishoba, Last Stand; worksheet reference gold: Opposition, Sterling Grove, Glittering Wish.

All cards below are exactly two colours (W + G, `color_count = 2`) and verified against the card database. Era is read from `printings` set codes. Older = better; post-2004 fits are flagged.

## Notes per pick

**Reclamation** (ICE, 1995) — A pure stax-lite hate enchantment: black creatures can't attack unless their controller sacrifices a land per attacker. In a low-power Sharpied pod with black aggro/midrange, this is a brutal soft-lock that costs the opponent their board OR their mana. Color-narrow by design, which keeps it fair. Perfect "lock the board" piece.

**Dueling Grounds** (INV, 2000) — "No more than one creature can attack each combat. No more than one creature can block each combat." The archetypal Selesnya pillowfort. It blunts every go-wide aggro deck, buys the control player infinite time, and is symmetric (your own token wall doesn't care because you're defending, not racing). No card advantage, so it never snowballs — exactly the honest lock this archetype wants.

**Hunting Grounds** (JUD, 2002) — A 2-mana enchantment that, once you hit threshold (7+ cards in graveyard), lets you flash a creature from hand onto the battlefield every time an opponent casts a spell. In a go-long deck this becomes a free-blocker / token-feeder engine that punishes the opponent for doing anything. Cheap, on-theme (graveyard-fueled, defensive), and slow enough to be fair.

**Kjeldoran Frostbeast** (ICE, 1995) — A 2/4 that destroys everything blocking or blocked by it at end of combat. A premier defensive board-control creature: it eats attackers (or trades into multiple blockers) and the 4 toughness lets it survive a lot. Honest because it must live through damage to trigger, and it's a removable creature.

**Femeref Enchantress** (VIS, 1997) — Draws a card whenever an enchantment hits your graveyard. The card-advantage glue for an enchantment-heavy control shell (alongside Reclamation, Dueling Grounds, Hunting Grounds, Sterling Grove). A 1/2 body keeps it fair; it rewards the deck's natural enchantment churn.

**Charging Troll** (INV, 2000) — A 3/3 with vigilance and {G}: regenerate. A sticky wall that blocks all day and can still poke in. Regeneration makes it hard to remove in combat, so it anchors the defensive board without being a bomb.

**Kei Takahashi** (LEG, 1994) — {T}: prevent the next 2 damage to a creature. A repeatable mini-fog/protection engine on a body. Slow and single-target, but in a grindy control game it neutralizes one combatant or burn spell per turn, extending the game toward your late-game win. Old-frame and very fair.

**Wheel of Sun and Moon** (SHM, 2008 — POST-2004 FLAG) — Cards that would go to the enchanted player's graveyard get bottomed instead. Primarily a survival/anti-mill and anti-recursion tool: enchant yourself to never deck out in a long grind, or enchant the opponent to shut off their graveyard recursion. Hybrid {G/W}, build-around, and purely defensive — fits the "win late" plan. Flagged as post-2004 (Shadowmoor).

**Nature's Blessing** (ALL, 1996) — {G}{W}, discard a card: put a +1/+1 counter on a creature or give it banding/first strike/trample, permanently. A slow, mana- and card-hungry grind engine that incrementally builds your wall or finishers over a long game. Banding even helps the control plan (great blocking math). Extremely fair; rewards patience.

**Asmira, Holy Avenger** (MIR, 1996) — A 2/3 flyer that grows by the number of your creatures that died this turn. In an attrition control deck where chump-blockers and tokens trade constantly, Asmira slowly becomes a real evasive finisher — an honest, removable, late-game payoff rather than a swingy bomb.

## Borderline / maybeboard

- **Oracle of Nectars** ({2}{G/W}, SHM 2008) — {X},{T}: gain X life. A repeatable lifegain sink to survive to the late game; hybrid, post-2004.
- **Heroes' Reunion** ({G}{W}, INV 2000) — gain 7 life. Pure stabilizer; thin on its own but cheap survival.
- **Vitalizing Cascade** ({X}{G}{W}, MIR 1996) — gain X+3 life. Scalable lifegain to buy turns; do-nothing-but-live.
- **Hymn of Rebirth** ({3}{G}{W}, ICE 1995) — reanimate any creature from any graveyard. A fair, slow value/finisher tool for a go-long deck.
- **Pollenbright Wings** ({4}{G}{W}, RAV 2005 — flag) — flying + makes a Saproling per point of combat damage. Token engine that needs a hitter; better in go-wide than pure control.
- **Aura Mutation** ({G}{W}, INV 2000) — destroy an enchantment, make Saprolings = its MV. Reactive answer + tokens; sideboard-y.
- **Wellspring** ({1}{G}{W}, MIR 1996) — repeatedly steal a land each upkeep; light tempo/control disruption, fragile.
