# {W}{U} Azorius — Self-Mill Enchantments

The deck mills/digs into its own library and grinds value from the graveyard and from enchantments: self-mill enablers, enchantment recursion, enchantments-matter glue, and slow pillowfort/control that buys time for an enchantment engine. NO swingy bombs, NO efficient "FIRE" cards.

## Why each fits

**Reviving Vapors** is the cleanest on-theme enabler here: it reveals the top three, keeps one (gaining its MV in life), and dumps the other two straight into your graveyard. That is exactly the self-mill-as-card-selection effect this deck wants — it fills the yard for Dance of the Manse / Cantivore while staying a fair, low-impact instant because you do not choose which two cards get binned.

**Phantatog** is a pure enchantments-matter payoff and a great decision-dense card: it turns spare enchantments (or hand cards) into incremental power. As a 1/2 it is harmless on its own and only matters once your enchantment count grows, which is precisely the slow-engine vibe — no bomb potential, lots of small choices.

**Hanna, Ship's Navigator** is the recursion backbone: {1}{W}{U}, tap to return an artifact or enchantment card from the graveyard to hand. In a low-power shell that is a grindy, taxing value loop rather than a combo — it rebuys your Auras and global enchantments one at a time, rewarding the self-mill plan without being oppressive.

**Reparations** rewards a defensive, enchantment-heavy posture with cards whenever an opponent targets you or your creatures. It is passive, opponent-dependent, and slow — perfect pillowfort glue that helps the enchantment deck out-grind aggression without ever being a swingy threat.

**Cloud Cover** is control/pillowfort glue that protects the engine: when an opponent targets one of your permanents, you may bounce it (dodging removal and re-triggering ETB enchanters). It generates no advantage on its own, so it is fair, but it keeps a slow enchantment build alive.

**Sawtooth Loon** doubles as a dig-and-fill creature: draw two, then bottom two, while bouncing one of your own white/blue creatures. The net effect is graveyard/library churn plus a re-usable ETB, all at a tempo cost — exactly the kind of fair, fiddly value the archetype wants.

**Guiding Spirit** is light graveyard interaction on a flyer: tap to recur the top creature of any graveyard onto its library. It is a one-card-per-turn trickle that supports the grind plan and is very low power, never threatening to take over a game.

**Silver Drake** is enabler glue — a 2/2 flyer whose ETB requires returning a white/blue creature you control, letting you re-fire value/enchanting ETB creatures. Cheap, fair, and synergistic with a rebuy-oriented enchantment shell.

## Borderline / maybeboard

- **Teferi's Moat** (Invasion, 2000) — strong pillowfort enchantment, but it is already in the cube.
- **Glaciers** (Ice Age, 1995) — old-frame WU enchantment, but its only effect (all Mountains are Plains) is near-blank outside hate decks; too marginal.
- **Spectral Shield / Chromatic Armor / Wings of Aesthir** (Ice Age) — period-correct WU Auras, but pure stat/protection Auras with no graveyard or enchantments-matter payoff; filler, not engine pieces.
- **Brago, King Eternal** (post-2004; Conspiracy 2014) — repeatable blink of your enchantments is very on-theme but strong and combo-adjacent; flag as too powerful for this very-low shell.
- **Monk Class** (post-2004; AFR 2021) — enchantment with light card-advantage, but new-frame and spell-velocity oriented rather than graveyard.
- **Niko Defies Destiny** (post-2004; KHM 2021) — Saga that recurs foretell cards; on-theme enchantment recursion but new and narrow.
- **Detention Sphere** (post-2004; RTR 2012) — clean removal enchantment, but more of a control staple than a self-mill/graveyard piece, and newer.
